I am an Associate Professor in the School of Information Science at the University of South Carolina. I work at the intersection of human centered AI, health informatics, social media analytics, and the science of science. I enjoy collaborating with students and colleagues across disciplines on projects that connect data, people, and society.

I lead the Hi Da Research Lab, where I work with undergraduate and graduate students and international collaborators. If you are interested in working with me as a co author, visiting scholar, or PhD student, feel free to contact me.

I also serve as Chair of the iSchool PhD program at the University of South Carolina and collaborate with initiatives such as the AI Peer Project and the USC Big Data Health Science Center.

My research uses data science and computational methods to study questions in health, scholarly communication, and online information behavior, including misinformation and disinformation. Project results appear in journals such as JAMA Network Open, Journal of Medical Internet Research, Information Processing and Management, Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology, and Journal of Systems and Software.

★ Teaching and research

I enjoy teaching and mentoring students at undergraduate, masters, and PhD levels. My teaching covers areas such as:

  • Data visualization and web analytics
  • User information behavior and information retrieval
  • Research methods and data analytics
  • Topics in human centered AI and health information

My research focuses on how people use information technologies and how data driven approaches can improve health, science, and communication systems. I am particularly interested in AI literacy, online misinformation, altmetrics, and the broader impacts of artificial intelligence on scholarly and public communication.

📰 News

  • Leading a large international survey across multiple countries to examine how people use generative AI, the benefits they see, the barriers they face, and how much they trust these tools.
  • Co author of a study analyzing hundreds of thousands of tweets about COVID 19. Using framing theory, we show how trending hashtags can reveal public frames during a health crisis and help track attention to those frames. The work appears in the Journal of Medical Internet Research.
  • Collaborating with the Office of the Vice President for Research at UofSC on a project evaluating the impact of NSF EPSCoR and NIH IDeA programs on research capacity and outcomes.
  • Co authored work accepted to the National Communication Association Annual Convention, Public Relations Division.
